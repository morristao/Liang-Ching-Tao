Selected research

01

Current research

Learning-Guided MCTS for Structured Sequential Decision-Making

How can search use a learned prior when the root action space is large, combinatorial, and subject to a limited simulation budget? This project combines graph-based policy representations with Monte Carlo Tree Search, examining action canonicalization, sampled-action search, evaluator design, and the empirical behavior of search itself.

  • Graph-based policy priors
  • MCTS under bounded computation
  • Action representation and canonicalization
  • Controlled evaluation and baselines
02

Master’s thesis

Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Charger Scheduling

My master’s work treats wireless rechargeable sensor networks as a demanding sequential optimization setting. A high-level Q-learning component selects regions or scheduling decisions, while force-aware routing determines charger movement. The methodological focus is hierarchical decomposition across coupled scheduling and routing decisions.

  • Hierarchical Q-learning
  • High-level scheduling
  • Low-level routing
  • Multi-charger decision-making
03

Research project

Neural-Guided Search for Graph-Structured Decisions

This work investigates neural guidance at the root and leaf of Monte Carlo Tree Search. It connects my work on hierarchical RL to current learning-guided planning by asking a practical question: when does a learned predictor improve the decisions reached by search, rather than only its own prediction score?

  • Root and leaf guidance
  • Prediction versus planning utility
  • Search guidance
  • Graph-structured decisions

About

Academic background

I am Liang-Ching Tao (陶亮清), based in Taiwan. My work began with reinforcement-learning approaches to multi-charger scheduling in wireless rechargeable sensor networks and progressed toward the more general problem of planning under large structured action spaces. At National Chung Hsing University, I have worked on hierarchical Q-learning, force-aware routing, neural-guided Monte Carlo Tree Search, and graph-based policy representations.

I am especially interested in research that evaluates learning and search as a joint system: through careful baselines, ablations, reproducible simulations, and attention to failure modes. In a PhD, I hope to build on this foundation in planning, sequential decision-making, adaptive agents, and reasoning.
