Planning & Search · Sequential Decision-Making · Reinforcement Learning

I am an M.S. graduate in Computer Science and Engineering from National Chung Hsing University, Taiwan, advised by Prof. Pi-Chung Wang. My research focuses on learning-guided planning: using learned policies and value functions to make explicit search effective in large, state-dependent action spaces. I use mobile charging and resource-allocation problems as structured testbeds for studying bounded planning, hierarchical decisions, and generalization across problem instances.

Looking ahead, I hope to extend this line of research toward agents that learn more of the structure required for planning—including dynamics, state and action abstractions, and hierarchical representations—while retaining explicit reasoning about future outcomes over long horizons under uncertainty. I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027.

Selected research

01

Ongoing · Sep. 2026–present

Learning-Guided Multi-Charger Planning with Neural-Guided Search

Building directly on LP-BTS, this ongoing extension moves from single-agent search toward coordinated multi-charger planning, where decisions interact through a combinatorial joint action space. It carries forward the same learning-guided planning principle while studying graph-based policy representations, canonical actions, action-space reduction, and neural-guided search for keeping coordinated lookahead tractable.

02

arXiv preprint · Sep. 2026

Learning-Guided Planning in Large Dynamic Action Spaces: Budgeted Tree Search for One-to-Many Mobile Charging

LP-BTS studies bounded planning when each decision is drawn from a large, changing action set. It combines a graph proposal policy and learned value critic with edge-budgeted PUCT, using learning to focus explicit lookahead rather than replace it. Because the policy scores candidates without a fixed output head, a single frozen checkpoint covers every evaluated setting, spanning action universes from 736 to 2,813 stops without retraining.

arXiv:2609.17429 ↗

03

Master’s thesis · arXiv preprint · 2025–2026

HQARRF: Hierarchical Q-learning and Force-aware Routing for Multi-Charger Scheduling

HQARRF studies hierarchical decision-making for multi-charger scheduling. A gated Q-learning upper level redirects service toward high-risk, under-served regions, while a force-aware lower-level ranker handles local routing and charging decisions—separating strategic allocation from fast structured action selection.

arXiv:2609.13901 ↗

About

Research perspective

Now. LP-BTS combines a graph proposal policy, a learned value critic, and edge-budgeted tree search. A single frozen checkpoint serves action universes from 736 to 2,813 stops without retraining, while matched ablations separate the contribution of learned candidate selection from explicit lookahead. This gives me a controlled setting for studying when learning and planning complement each other.

Near term. I am extending the framework to several chargers through canonical actions and action-space reduction, with the goal of making coordinated planning tractable as the joint decision space grows.

Longer term. These projects lead to a broader question: how can explicit planning remain effective as more of the decision structure itself becomes learned? I am particularly interested in learned dynamics and world models, state and action abstractions, hierarchical representations, and planning under uncertainty. LP-BTS provides a concrete starting point: replace its current simulator with a learned dynamics model and study how model error, search budget, and transfer across problem instances interact. More broadly, I want to build agents that learn useful internal models and use them to predict, reason, and plan over long horizons.
