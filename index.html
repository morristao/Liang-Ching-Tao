I am Liang-Ching Tao, an M.S. graduate in Computer Science and Engineering from National Chung Hsing University in Taiwan. I study AI planning and sequential decision-making: how learned policies and neural representations can guide search—particularly Monte Carlo Tree Search—when decisions are large, structured, and computation is limited.

I am preparing for Fall 2027 PhD applications in AI planning, sequential decision-making, reinforcement learning, and learning-guided search.

01

Current research · September 2026–present

Learning-Guided Multi-Charger Planning with Neural-Guided Search

Developing a learning-guided planning framework for multi-charger scheduling that combines graph-based policy representations with neural-guided search over large, structured action spaces. The work investigates canonical actions, action-space reduction, and sampled-action ideas for allocating limited search computation more effectively, while treating multi-agent coordination as a possible extension rather than a completed MARL system.

  • Learning-guided planning
  • Graph-based policy representations
  • Canonical actions and action-space reduction
  • Sampled-action search under bounded computation
02

Research project · 2026–present

Neural-Guided MCTS for Single-Charger WRSN Scheduling

This work established the neural-guided search foundation for my current planning direction. It evaluates root- and leaf-level neural guidance in Monte Carlo Tree Search and asks whether learned predictors improve end-to-end planning utility under bounded computation, rather than only standalone prediction scores.

  • Root and leaf guidance
  • End-to-end planning utility
  • Search under bounded computation
  • Neural representations for planning
03

Master’s thesis

Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Charger Scheduling

My master’s work treats wireless rechargeable sensor networks as a demanding sequential optimization setting. A high-level Q-learning component selects regions or scheduling decisions, while force-aware routing determines charger movement. The methodological focus is hierarchical decomposition across coupled scheduling and routing decisions.

  • Hierarchical Q-learning
  • High-level scheduling
  • Low-level routing
  • Multi-charger decision-making

About

Research perspective

My research began with multi-charger scheduling in wireless rechargeable sensor networks, where coupled routing, charging, and allocation decisions made hierarchical decomposition essential. This setting led me from reinforcement learning and force-aware routing to neural-guided Monte Carlo Tree Search and graph-based policy representations.

The question that now motivates my work is not simply whether a learned component predicts well, but whether it improves the decisions reached by a planner. I study learning and search as a joint system through end-to-end planning utility, controlled baselines, ablations, reproducible simulations, and attention to failure modes.
